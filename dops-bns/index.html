Your AI answer

The three layer AI framework

Carried over from your KPMG prep and adapted to this role. The order is the point: team, then system, then experience. The system layer is your headline, and it connects to the Design Systems partnership named in the job description.

Layer 1

Team layer

What designers, researchers and content designers do every day
  • Research synthesis, variant generation, accessibility checks, copy drafts, design QA.
  • Most teams start here. The return is fast and the risk is contained.
  • The trap is treating it as the whole story.
Layer 2 · headline

System layer

Tokenized, machine readable, componentized design system
  • AI can generate variants that stay on standard, flag inconsistencies and keep accessibility rules in the loop.
  • The hardest layer to build, the one that compounds, and the one most organizations skip.
  • In this role you would partner closely with Design Systems.
Layer 3

Experience layer

Personalization, dynamic content, conversational interfaces
  • Highest customer impact and highest risk, especially where trust and regulation set the bar.
  • Go here once the system layer is sound, not before.
  • Teams often jump from layer 1 to layer 3 because that is where the headlines are.

Say it out loud (about 90 seconds)

“My honest take is that AI in design is roughly where mobile was in 2008. Everyone is talking about it, most teams use it tactically, and very few treat it as infrastructure. I look at it in three layers, and the order matters.

The first is the team layer: what designers, researchers and content designers do every day. Research synthesis, variants, accessibility checks, copy drafts. Most teams start here because the return is fast and the risk is contained. The trap is treating it as the whole story.

The second is the system layer. If the design system is tokenized and machine readable, AI can work against it and stay on standard. It is the hardest layer to build, it is the one that compounds, and most organizations skip it. In this role I would work closely with Design Systems on it.

The third is the experience layer: personalization, dynamic content, conversational interfaces. Highest customer impact and highest risk, especially in wealth management where trust and regulation set the bar. You go there once the system layer is sound.

Around all three sits the operational work this role owns: guidance on approved tools and data, enablement, measurement, and human oversight, with risk and compliance partners in from the start. I see this as a framework to test against what you are already seeing, not a finished answer.”
