The job: rebuild a 12-year-old, transaction-heavy banking legacy platform

Used monthly by more than 2.5M customers that could not afford a single day of disruption to their financial lives. This project was compared to rebuilding a plane while it is in flight, our challenge was to design a sustainable digital channel operation without affecting revenue and service, way beyond just an interface revamp.

For most of our 2.5M unique web customers, banking wasn't occasional, it was a weekly, sometimes daily activity. The platform had shaped their mental model of banking itself. We were about to change everything underneath them, and most of them didn't want change.

The human dimension: what customers taught us before we wrote a line of code

Three discovery findings set the architecture:

  • Mobile-first wasn't a tech call, it was an observed reality. Transaction initiation had already shifted to mobile and tablets.
  • The beta program was change management, not QA. We recruited our highest-anxiety segments to validate that task familiarity transferred, not just that the system worked.
  • Zero tolerance for confusion on critical paths. Bill payments and e-transfers were stress-tested end-to-end before any customer saw them. Crafting consistent UX copy was a must.

12 years of accumulated constraints

Limitations on speed to market, CX consistency, accessibility, and development process agility. Design teams kept re-solving the same problems across squads, without leverage.

Two non-negotiables:

  • Migrate 2.5M customers without disrupting a single daily-critical task.
  • Reduce friction across Product, Design, and Engineering without slowing the release train.

The goals we set

  • Modernize aging codebase and tech stack.
  • Cohesive and scalable UX.
  • Accelerate speed to market.
  • Build a future-ready foundation for personalization, experimentation and metrics tracking.

What we built

Design craft stance: keep the mental model, rebuild everything else

On a platform where people move real money, familiarity is trust. We preserved and modernized legacy patterns and task groupings, made them cleaner, accessible, and progressively disclosed. Migration felt natural, not too disruptive.

The mobile-first decision was contentious: engineering wanted web-first, the legacy system's muscle memory. I pushed back. A "future-ready" platform couldn't have a web-first bias. We designed for mobile constraints, then expanded outward. That decision reshaped the micro-app architecture and the way we called core banking systems.

Over 2+ years, Design, Product, and Engineering shipped a responsive platform built from modular micro-apps: one system, multiple devices.

My role as Head of Design was to chart a strategic path for a scalable digital experience rooted in design principles, that aligned business goals, enabled predictable system evolution, and documented decisions and guidelines for future development.

How our strategic design approach solved it: 4 pillars

Design Strategy

  • Design principles to anchor all platform decisions
  • Mobile-first approach, aligned with business strategy
  • Joint CX governance with Product Management
  • Guidance on building web vs. native decisions

Experience Architecture

  • Modular, reusable, responsive UI components built into design system
  • Develop once, deploy anywhere: including mobile-embedded (headless) experiences
  • Single information architecture across devices
  • Accessibility compiled into code libraries
  • Beta program structured as change management, not QA

Operating Model

  • Specialized support for every squad building on web
  • Governance without bottlenecks: managed through reference docs, update notifications, design jams, and critiques replaced mandatory sign-offs
  • Clear ownership for every platform component: navigation, payments, rewards, settings, product configuration

Platform Thinking

  • Design as infrastructure: foundational systems, tools, and processes enabling consistency, efficiency, and scale
  • Reusable code libraries to accelerate deployment
  • Curation model to separate new patterns from evolution of existing ones

What the platform unlocked

  • Expanded self-serve capabilities
  • A tokenized design system, prerequisite for AI-driven theming and personalization
  • Foundation and discovery for customer-managed privacy and PII controls
  • Always-on experimentation module baked in
  • A foundation ready for AI use cases now on the roadmap: personalization, anomaly detection, product recommendations, image-based validation, predictive analytics, content generation, and customer-support agents

Tension story: governance as guide not gate

When we launched CX governance, squads pushed back. A PM told us flatly: "Your reviews will slow our velocity". He had a point, our first version was too process-heavy. We reframed governance from gate to guide. Within a quarter, squads were reaching out and adopting voluntarily, because it saved rework, not because we imposed a model. Mandate creates compliance. Co-creation creates adoption.

Cleaner, but not without friction: customers faced a learning curve on the new platform

We slowed down, ran targeted discovery to find where confidence broke, and reprioritized roadmaps for findability and to enable customer recovery. The +12% engagement lift came directly out of that first loop, with our customers help we've implemented measures for recovery, help and findability, and engagement keeps growing.

Cost avoidance mattered to the business, but the headline is this: 2.5 million people every month kept managing their financial products uninterrupted. In banking, trust is the product. We didn't just modernize a platform, we protected the customer relationship at a moment it was most exposed.
